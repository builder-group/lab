# Design Language

MidiMarble should feel like a native Mac app that people already know how to use. When a pattern exists in an established app, use it instead of inventing one. Innovation belongs in the musical track building, not in basic controls.

Paper (paper.design) is the main reference for layout and interaction: document tabs in the title bar, a full-height left sidebar, a narrow vertical tool rail, a compact right inspector and almost no chrome. Its natural, paper-like look is not copied; surfaces follow Apple Photos and native iOS and macOS, with the yellow accent. The timeline at the bottom borrows behaviour from video editors and Blender and is drawn in the same native style.

Blender is the main interaction reference, because the film is finished in Blender and the app should feel like a friendly front end to it: its timeline and dope sheet for time, its properties editor for structured settings, and its shortcuts where they fit (G, R, Tab, numpad framing). The surface is Apple-native rather than Blender's custom toolkit: system font, translucent sidebar, native menus and sheets. Figma is the reference for canvas navigation and selection.

Reference apps:

| App                   | Patterns to borrow                                                   |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Blender               | Timeline and dope sheet, properties sections, keyframe vocabulary, G/R shortcuts, framing |
| Logic Pro, GarageBand | Lanes, playhead, transport, timeline zoom, Space to play             |
| Final Cut Pro         | Workspace switching, inspector, clips moved on a timeline            |
| Figma, FigJam         | Canvas navigation, dot grid, selection, scrubbable fields, components |
| Notion, Linear        | Command palette, keyboard flow, calm visuals                         |
| Blender               | Frame shortcuts for users who know it                                |
| Xcode                 | Start window with templates, activity view for long jobs             |

## Principles

- **Best parts, not copies:** take the strongest pattern for each job from Paper, Figma, video editors, Blender and native macOS apps such as GarageBand, Logic and Photos, then make them read as one coherent native app. No reference is copied one to one

- **Little text:** labels stay short and explanations live behind a small question-mark icon with a tooltip, so panels stay compact. Icons are fine wherever they are unambiguous and carry a tooltip with the shortcut
- **Figma-like panels:** the sidebar reads like Figma's layers panel and the inspector like Figma's properties panel: compact rows, clear hierarchy, no prose
- **A real timeline:** the timeline behaves like a video editor or Blender's timeline and dope sheet: tracks with headers, a ruler with a draggable playhead, keyframe-style hits, transport in its header

- **Playback looks like a video editor:** play, pause, previous and next hit and the time sit together in the timeline header, as in Final Cut and Premiere. The play triangle is reserved for playback; no other control uses a glyph that could be mistaken for it

- **Familiar first:** a new user should recognize every control from another app
- **Native:** system font, menus, context menus, light and dark appearance, standard shortcuts
- **Canvas first:** panels stay compact and quiet so the track and music lead
- **Keyboard and direct manipulation:** every frequent action has a shortcut and can also be done by dragging
- **Honest feedback:** timing, misses and stale previews are always visible, never hidden behind a check button
- **Progressive depth:** the default path needs no Blender knowledge; depth appears through the inspector, Blender and agents

Names, labels and every other string follow [words.md](words.md), which takes precedence over wording elsewhere in this document.

## Round 5 decisions: Scene, skins in the inspector, sound in the Song, themes as files

From the owner's hands-on check on 2026-11-04. These take precedence over Round 4 and older sections where they differ.

- **The Scene is selectable.** Layers starts with a **Scene** row, like Blender's Scene Collection; its inspector replaces the Project inspector (Length, Marble Speed, Seamless Loop, Theme), named Scene as Blender names frame range and gravity. Clicking empty space in Layers, on the wall or preview, or pressing Esc selects the Scene. Nothing can be left "stuck" on a selection
- **The Library holds what you add; the inspector holds how the selection looks.** Compose's Library is pieces to place, Film's Library is Decor to place: both drag or double-click into the scene. Skins are not added things, so they leave the Library: selecting a marble, piece or the Wall shows its skins right in the inspector's Look section as visual tiles with their properties below, and Reset to Theme. Themes leave the Library too: the Scene inspector's Theme section shows the current theme and its parts, with a picker
- **Music comes only from the Song; hits only click.** There are two kinds of sound and they never mix. The **Song** is the music: a MIDI file or an audio file, and it is what the film carries. **Hit Clicks** is an editing aid, like Logic's metronome: a neutral short tick when a marble lands a hit, with a toggle in the transport, on by default, never in the exported film; it lets you hear hits against an MP3 while syncing them by hand. A marble plays nothing and has no Instrument. A project is about one song: MIDI hits come mostly automatically from its notes, audio hits are made by hand (tapped or placed), and a hit has a Pitch only when it comes from a MIDI note. A project without a song still works, with only clicks, and the hit summary nudges Import Song… before Export Film…; there is no generated "Tapped Notes" song. Mute and solo live on the Song: MIDI's notes are split across marbles, so the Song row expands to its **Parts** (one per marble that plays notes, coloured by marble, plus Backing), each with Sound, mute and solo, which is how you listen to one marble's slice. Audio has one mute and solo on the Song row. Marble rows have none
- **Compose's pieces belong to the app; Film's look belongs to the project; themes are files.** Hitbox pieces are fixed to the app. A theme is a preset of parts (wall, lights, a skin per element, marble look, Decor set) that travels as a file: **Export Theme…** and **Import Theme…** in the Scene inspector, assets included. A few starter themes ship as ordinary theme files, treated like imported ones, not as a special built-in kind. Seasonal looks such as Christmas are not built in: Christmas lives in the Jingle Bells example project and can be exported from it. Skins and Decor items used by a project are copied into it, so a project is always self-contained

## Round 4 decisions: Layers, each layer owns its settings

From the owner's review on 2026-11-04. These take precedence over Round 2 and Round 3 and the older sections below where they differ.

- **One word: Layers.** The sidebar outline and the timeline rows are the same objects, like After Effects, where each layer has a row in the timeline. "Tracks" and "track" are gone from the UI: the timeline's column header reads Layers, Quick Help names the region Timeline, and tooltips and hints say layer or row. Reason: two words for one object made people look for a difference that does not exist; one outline already drives both places, so it gets one name. Code keeps its internal names (`TrackHeader`, `data-track`)
- **Compose is the performance, Film is the presentation.** Layers and the timeline in Compose show only the Song (when present) and the marbles with their pieces, plus Pieces. Camera with its shots and Titles appear only in Film, which shows every layer; the Song and marbles stay selectable there for skins. Camera framing problems found in Compose stay in the hit summary and jump to Film with the Camera layer selected. Reason: Compose is about notes, hits and the run; camera and titles are how the film presents it
- **Each layer owns its settings.** The inspector shows the selected layer's settings, as a title already did. The Song layer holds the file, tempo and meter, Backing with its Sound, Replace Song… and Delete Song. The Camera layer holds the Frame and every camera setting (Automatic or Manual, distance, pitch, lead, smoothing, the framing problem at the playhead, Add Shot, Reset Camera); it shows when the Camera is selected in either workspace. Shots, titles and marbles keep their own inspectors; several titles selected (the Titles group or the Titles row header) show the titles with Add Title. The Project inspector (nothing selected) keeps only project-wide settings: Length with Fit to Song and Marble Speed, whose help says it changes every marble; Film adds the film's Look (theme, colours, quality, loop), which belongs to no layer. Reason: settings were split between the layer and a Project panel that mixed song, camera and physics, so people did not know where to look
- **Song is optional.** The Song layer exists only while the project has a song file or Backing notes; a new or tapped project shows no Song row, in Layers or the timeline. The Add menu (+) offers **Add Song…** (Import Song…) while there is none, and drops Replace and Delete, which live on the Song layer. Delete (⌫, the context menu, the inspector) removes the song file and Backing as one undo step with a notice and Undo; the marble hits stay. A confirmation alert ("Delete Jingle Bells.mid and its 40 Backing notes?" · Cancel · Delete) appears only when Backing notes would be lost. The empty wall keeps Import Song… and Tap Along. Reason: an empty Song row in every new project read as a missing step, and tapped projects never need one
- **Selection is one selection.** Selecting a layer in the timeline selects it in Layers and in the inspector and the other way round, for the Song, each marble, pieces, the Camera, each shot, the Titles and each title; the other side scrolls to it
- **Everything visible is a layer; the Project holds what is not** (owner, 2026-11-04; built with the skins work). A layer is a thing in the film you can select, that has its own settings, can exist several times, can carry a skin or be timed. Film's Layers, in a fixed order by kind like Blender's outliner: **Song** (optional; the file as its subtitle), each **marble** with its pieces and its Finish (marbles stay top-level, no Marbles folder, so pieces sit one level deep), **Pieces**, **Wall** (its skin, such as plaster, pegboard or a backdrop, its colour and the background behind it), **Lights** (a group: the theme's Key Light plus any added with + › Light, each with colour, strength and direction), **Camera** (one layer with its shots inside), **Titles** (a group; their order is their stacking on screen and can be dragged, as in Figma), **Decor** (a group, below). Depth decides what is in front in the 3D film, so the order by kind is fixed and never needs thought. The Project (nothing selected) keeps only what cannot be seen: Length with Fit to Song, Marble Speed, Seamless Loop and the Theme; Quality lives only in the Export sheet, Colour by Note becomes a skin property, and the Wall, Light and Background colours move to their layers. A layer the theme dresses shows "From Brass" with an override, like a piece's skin. Compose keeps showing only the Song and the marbles with their pieces. Built without backdrops: the Wall's Skin is a row of the themes' wall kinds for now, and Wall and Lights have no timeline row
- **Skins have properties** (owner, 2026-11-04). Like Shopify theme settings: every skin has a few standard properties with the same names on every skin, **Colour** (Skin, Marble, By Note), **On Hit** (None, Press, Wobble, Glow), **Glow Colour** (with Glow) and **Variant** (where the skin has variants), at most about five in all; agent-made skins may add their own in their manifest (Ribbon Colour on a gift box). The theme sets every property's default ("Neon" means platforms glow in their note's colour on hit); a selection overrides them for its pieces, and a mixed selection shows the properties its skins share. Marbles have skins too: Glass (a play marble with a swirl inside), Metal (a chrome ball bearing), Magic (glass holding drifting coloured smoke, like codrops' "Magical Marbles in Three.js")
- **Pieces have hitboxes, Decor never does; the workspace decides** (owner, 2026-11-04). Everything placed in Compose is a piece (platform, rail, tunnel, switchback, windmill, Finish) and always collides: that is what makes it a piece. A piece a marble plays or follows sits under that marble; any other piece sits in a group named **Pieces** (was Free Pieces), with a ? "Pieces no marble plays or follows; marbles can still hit them". Everything added in Film is Decor and never collides. Something decorative the marble should bounce on is a piece with a skin, placed in Compose, so its timing is checked. The inspector shows a link panel only for a linked piece (Plays Note 4, Follows Melody at 0:03.2, with Detach); an unlinked piece shows none, and Play a Note… and Follow a Marble… are in its context menu and ⌘K. "Advanced" is split by what it holds: **Hitbox** (Arms, Arm Length, Arm Width, Centre Size, Runs, Run Height, Turn Gap, Guard, Turn Curve, Thickness, a Finish's Size and Run-Out) and **Physics** (Brake, Spin, bounce and friction where a piece has them), both collapsed by default
- **A piece has a hitbox and a skin, like Unity's collider and mesh** (owner, 2026-11-04). The piece keeps its name (Platform, Rail, Windmill…); its **Hitbox** is what a marble touches and is shaped in Compose, its **Skin** is how it looks and is set in Film; Decor has a skin and no hitbox. Compose draws pieces as their hitboxes, the inspector's shape panel is named **Hitbox** (Arms, Arm Length, Run Height and the like) beside a collapsed Physics panel, and Quick Help says "Compose: hitboxes and timing". Film draws skins; a selected piece shows its hitbox as a thin outline, and **View › Show Hitboxes** outlines all of them, like Unity's collider gizmos, so a skin that looks bigger than what the marble hits is visible. Quick Help says "Film: skins, camera and titles". Skin properties never change a hitbox
- **Decor** (owner, 2026-11-04; built, see ux.md Decor, without the Christmas theme). Decorations sit in the Decor group, one layer each, Film only. Decor never has physics, so it can never change a hit; overlapping a marble's path shows the amber "Overlaps Melody's path" with Move Clear, and **Scatter Decor** fills only the free wall the camera shows, beside the run and clear of every path. Something a marble should bounce on is a piece with a skin, not decor. Decor can have In and Out like a title. The agent makes decor, skins, backdrops and marble skins per project from a sentence or a point on the wall ("Christmas decor along the sides" returns a set and scatters it); accepted items land in the project's Library, the star keeps them for every project. Anything added directly in Blender is Decor too: it sits in the same group with the origin line "Made in Blender", hides, shows and takes In and Out like the rest, Open in Blender jumps to it, its bounds still drive the path warning, and the Draft Preview draws a faint outline with "Shows in the Blender preview". First target: a built-in Christmas theme made only of parts that also work alone (Gift platform skin, Glass marbles, a tinsel backdrop, a scattered bauble and snowflake set) for Jingle Bells
- **A theme dresses everything; a skin overrides one piece** (owner, 2026-11-04; built after the Finish piece). Like Tailwind's theme and utility classes: the project has one theme, a preset of parts (the scene: wall, light, background; a skin for every piece type: platform, rail, tunnel, switchback, windmill, start, finish; the marble look; the title style). Every element that can be placed has its own set of skins, usable in any theme, so skins carry no theme in their identity. A piece without a skin of its own shows its theme's skin for its type; selecting pieces and picking a skin overrides only them, and Reset to Theme brings the theme's skin back. The theme inspector lists its parts (Wall, Light, Platforms, Rails, Tunnels, Switchbacks, Windmills, Marbles, Titles), each opening the matching skins in the Library; Save as Theme turns the current mix into a project theme. A medley needs no second theme: the project starts from one theme, and the pieces of a later part of the song get other skins (select a time range of pieces, pick a skin). Reason: the owner wants one general look that dresses the whole film and the freedom to restyle any element, as in the reference short whose song parts change look

## Round 3 decisions: one outline, one library, themes that dress everything

From the owner's review on 2026-11-03 night. Goal: films at the level of the reference channels (virtual3dworld_1000, marvinblend), made reliably by beginners and power users alike. Compose is where both work the same way; Film is where they differ: a beginner picks a theme or asks the agent, a power user opens Blender. Wording follows [words.md](words.md).

- **Title bar carries the project's actions.** Left: sidebar toggle, Home. Centre: title. Right: the Blender status chip, Quick Help (?), Agent, **Export Film…** (the one accent button, the only export button in the window), panel toggles. The Blender chip shows its state with a dot and a word (Ready, Starting, Rendering Preview, Not Installed, Error) and opens a menu: Open in Blender, Restart Blender, Show Blender Log. The inspector header keeps only the zoom
- **One outline in both workspaces.** The sidebar's Layers tree is the outline of the whole project: Song, each Marble with the pieces it plays or that follow it in hit order, Pieces, Camera with its shots, Titles. Rows that also have a timeline track carry a small track glyph; selecting a row selects its track and the other way round. The timeline's track column is the timed part of the same outline, in the same order. One Add menu (+) serves both places: Marble, Piece ▸, Title, Shot, Song…
- **Sidebar tabs: Layers and Library**, like Figma's Layers and Assets. The Library is one browser in both workspaces, filtered by what you are doing: in Compose it lists pieces to place (the Insert popover is its quick form), in Film it lists themes and skins to dress the run
- **Themes dress everything.** A theme is a complete look: wall, light, marble and a default skin for every piece type, plus the variants it offers per type (Brass: Gold, Copper, Silver, Device, Key, Knob platforms). Picking a theme card restyles the film. A theme has options that change the whole look at once, such as **Colour by Note** (each platform takes its note's colour, like boomwhackers). Power users duplicate a theme to change its options (Duplicate Theme makes "Neon Copy" with colour wells for Wall, Light, Glow and Background; built-in themes stay read-only with Duplicate to Edit); the agent can make a new theme from a sentence ("New Theme with Agent…"). Project themes sit among the theme cards with a Based on Neon or Made with Agent origin line, never in a grouping of their own
- **Skins follow the selection.** With a platform selected, the Library shows platform skins: the current theme's variants first, then skins made with the agent or imported. Click applies to the selection, ⌥-click to every piece of that type. With nothing selected it shows themes
- **No storage words.** There is no "In This Project" or "My Library". Every item shows where it comes from as a quiet line (Brass Theme, Made with Agent, Imported) and an In Use badge when the project uses it. A star (**Keep in Library**) makes an item available in every project; items a project uses are copied into it silently so it stays portable
- **Blender for power users, in place.** Open in Blender opens the scene file; edits made there show in Layers as a Detached glyph and in the inspector as one row with Reset to Theme. Nothing else in the app talks about Blender internals

## Round 2 decisions

From the owner's review of the open-canvas build on 2026-11-03. These take precedence over older sections below until those are rewritten to match.

- **Transport like Logic and Final Cut.** The timeline header holds one transport group of plain glyph buttons (Go to Start, Previous Hit, Play or Pause, Next Hit) with no filled circle behind Play, then an LCD-style time readout in tabular numerals (current time large, length secondary; click to type a time). Zoom sits at the right as a Final Cut zoom slider with a fit button. One icon family and stroke weight throughout; Previous and Next Hit use step glyphs that cannot be mistaken for Play
- **Track headers like GarageBand.** A marble's track header shows the colour chip, the name and the instrument, and icon buttons for Mute (speaker) and Solo (headphones) with tooltips, never bare "M" and "S" letters. A marble's track stays one compact row of hits. Only the Song layer with notes expands like Logic's piano roll: a keyboard strip in the header column and one row per semitone, notes that drag in time and in pitch
- **The song is project-wide.** A film has one soundtrack: the imported MIDI file or recording, with its tempo. It appears as a Song track at the top of the timeline (waveform for audio, a mini note overview for MIDI) with its file name, and is imported, replaced or removed from the timeline header's add menu or by dropping a file on the timeline. Marbles play parts of it: each marble's track holds the hits it strikes. The World inspector shows Song (file, tempo) and Film (frame, length with Fit to Song); no import buttons hide in a panel named Music
- **Inspector like Figma UI3.** Section titles flush left with no indented disclosure column; fields in a two-column grid with the label inside the field (X, Y, angle, length), aligned to one 8 px grid across sections. No prose in panels; every explanation is a question-mark tooltip. Status shows as a pill in the inspector header (On Time, 12 ms Late, Missed, Stopped, Blocked, Open) instead of a Timing section
- **Relations navigate.** An attachment row ("Hit 1 · A4 · 0:00.36") is a link that selects that hit; the hit inspector edits the note (pitch, velocity, time) and shows "Played by Platform 1" as a link back, or an Add Platform action when nothing plays it. Detaching is one icon button on the row. There is no separate pin: a linked platform follows its hit, an unlinked one stays where it is
- **One path tool, two path pieces.** The Path tool draws a Bézier path; the path becomes a rail by default and a segmented control in the inspector switches it between Rail and Tunnel, keeping its shape. There is no separate tunnel tool; U arms the Path tool with the tunnel type, so a tunnel can be drawn directly
- **Insert from a library.** The tool rail keeps Move, Hand, Marble, Platform and Path, plus an Insert (+) button that opens a popover of pieces with a small preview and a one-line tooltip each (Windmill, Rail, Tunnel, Switchback and others), like Figma's assets or Paper's insert panel. Click arms the piece, dragging a tile drops it on the canvas
- **Agent as a shared chat.** The agent lives behind a title bar button that opens a chat panel, one conversation per project. The current selection appears in the composer as @-mention chips and typing @ mentions any layer. Agent progress collapses to one status line that expands on demand; results come back as cards with a preview and Accept or Discard. No agent panel in the inspector and no explanatory subtext
- **Songs start in the timeline.** An empty project shows an empty Song track as the drop target with Import Song…, not a card over the canvas. The document that was open at quit or reload opens again, like macOS window restoration
- **One project per window.** The title bar shows the document title with its edited state, Home and the panel toggles; there is no tab strip until several projects can really be open. Blender's connection state sits beside Export, where it matters (superseded in round 3: both moved to the title bar)
- **Layers like Figma.** 28 px rows, one 16 px indent per level, a small type icon, the name, and state glyphs only where they carry information (link or anchor), hover actions on the right

- Title bar: one project per window, so there is no tab strip. The traffic lights keep their 72 px inset, then the sidebar toggle and Home (house), which returns to the start window. The document title sits centred like a macOS document window, with a red "Not Saved" when saving failed and a double-click to rename. A saved project autosaves within a second of each edit, so it shows no edited dot (it only flickered); the tooltip says it saves automatically. An unsaved document such as an opened example shows no dot (its autosave is internal) and a quiet secondary "Not Saved" once it has changes, like the Edited of an untitled macOS document. On the right, the project's actions, the same in Compose and Film: the Blender chip, the Quick Help button (a ? glyph, ⇧⌘/, accent fill while on), the Agent button (sparkles and label, ⌘J; a small accent dot while an agent works and the chat is closed), **Export Film…** (⌘E), then the timeline and inspector toggles. Toggles say Show or Hide in their native tooltip
- Blender chip: a quiet pull-down like Xcode's run destination, a 7 px dot and one word: Ready (green; grey while installed but not started), Starting or Rendering Preview (pulsing amber), Not Installed or Error (red). The tooltip has the detail with the version or the error. In the browser it reads Blender with a hollow dot and "Blender connects in the desktop app", an expected state rather than a problem. Its menu repeats the detail as a header, then Open in Blender, Restart Blender and Show Blender Log (the worker log in Finder, once a worker ran); without Blender it holds only Install Blender…, which opens blender.org in the browser
- Export Film… is the window's one accent button and the only export button. While a film exports with the sheet closed it carries the progress as a small ring before its label, like Final Cut's background tasks; a finished or failed export leaves a green or red dot until the sheet is opened again. The tooltip names the step ("Rendering frame 120 of 600") and a click shows the sheet
- Agent chat: a 360 px panel at the right edge of the window, full height beside the inspector and the timeline, so the canvas keeps its width minus the panel and nothing covers it. In a window narrower than 1600 px, opening the chat hides the inspector and closing it brings the inspector back, like Xcode's inspector auto-hide; showing the inspector while the chat is open keeps both
- Sidebar, full height and translucent like Finder and Photos: the project name (double-click renames), the Compose and Film segmented control, then two tabs, **Layers** and **Library**, like Figma UI3's Layers and Assets: plain 11 px semibold words, the open one in a quiet pill, and on the Layers tab the Add menu (+) at the right end of the row. Each workspace remembers its tab across launches. Library in Compose is the Insert library's tiles in two columns (Paths, then Pieces and Marble): a click arms the tool, a drag drops the piece on the wall, the armed tile has an accent ring. Library in Film is the theme and skin browser. Show Library in the command palette opens Film's Library tab
- **Add menu (+):** one menu in two places, the Layers tab row and the timeline's Layers column header: Add Marble, Add Piece ▸ (Rail, Tunnel, Platform, Windmill, Switchback with their keys; each arms its tool), Add Title, Add Shot (at the playhead, ⇧M), then Add Song… while the project has no Song layer. Replace Song… and Delete Song live on the Song layer (context menu and inspector). An item of the other workspace opens it first: Add Piece from Film opens Compose with the tool armed, Add Title and Add Shot from Compose open Film. Submenus open on hover, click or Right Arrow, like macOS
- Layers is the outline of the whole project, in Compose the Song and the marbles with their pieces, in Film every layer, drawn like Figma UI3's layer list: 28 px rows, a 16 px disclosure column, one 16 px indent per level, a 14 px type icon and the name. In order: the **Song** (only while the project has a song file or Backing notes, a note or waveform icon and the file name), each **Marble** (its colour chip) as a group with the pieces linked or anchored to it in hit order, **Pieces** as a folder group, newest first, **Camera** with its shots by time (kind and time), and **Titles** as a folder group. Empty Pieces and Titles groups are left out. Layers with a row in the timeline (Song, marbles, Camera and Titles in Film) carry a small timeline glyph, a line with a keyframe diamond, in the trailing column; selecting a layer in Layers selects its timeline row and the inspector, and the other way round, and the other side scrolls to it. Camera, shots and titles belong to Film and are listed there only. A click on a folder group selects its children. The quiet trailing column holds only information: problem badges, the hit time of a linked piece or a shot in 11 px tabular figures (0:03.2), an anchor glyph for anchored pieces and the track glyph. On a piece row hover gives way to the lock and eye toggles, which stay visible while on. Hover draws a hairline outline; selected rows are tinted, the children of a selected marble or the Camera and a marble whose hits are selected get a lighter tint, and adjacent highlighted rows join into one block. Hidden and locked are view state. Click selects, ⌘-click toggles, Shift-click selects a range, double-click or Return renames marbles, pieces and titles, Up and Down move the selection, Left and Right fold a group
- Tool rail, 48 px, between sidebar and canvas: Move and Hand, then the workspace's tools. Compose: Marble, Platform, Path, then Insert (+), which opens the Insert library popover beside the rail. Film: Move and Title. The rail holds tools only, so Film's Library is the sidebar tab and not a rail button (round 3 review, finding 25). Tooltips open to the right with the name, one line of help and the key
- Inspector: in Compose a top bar with only the zoom and its menu (zoom, fit, Snap and Follow); Film has no zoom and no top bar. Then the header row and sections for the selection, or the Project without one
- Timeline: spans canvas and inspector at the bottom. Its header holds the transport like Logic's control bar, centred: Go to Beginning, Previous Hit, Play, Next Hit and the LCD time readout, then Final Cut's zoom slider at the far end
- Nothing floats over the canvas except feedback and the contextual build bar: the Snap and Follow toggles sit in its top right corner
- Sheets, the ⌘K palette and context menus are unchanged
- A start window offers New Project, Import Song… (one panel for a MIDI file or a recording) and Open Project…, recent projects and examples grouped as Songs and Techniques. Each example shows a 28 × 48 px portrait still of its run rendered in Blender (`public/examples/<id>.jpg`, 160 px wide), like Xcode's and GarageBand's template pickers; a recent project shows its folder, and nothing in the browser.

The inspector follows Figma UI3's properties panel. A 56 px header row holds a 32 px icon tile, the name (click renames it in place), the type below it and, where it applies, a status pill on the right. Sections have flush-left 11 px semibold titles with a chevron that appears on hover and collapses them, hairline separators and 16 px side padding; a section without content shows only its title row, like Figma's Export section. Fields sit on one two-column grid with 8 px gaps and carry their label inside: X, Y, ∠ for the angle, L for length, or a short word such as Pitch, Frame or Skin, with the full name in the tooltip. Open and closed sections are remembered per section.

- Piece: Path (a Rail | Tunnel segmented control for drawn paths, switching the type in one undo step while the path, pose or anchor and a custom name stay; a default name such as Rail 2 is renumbered after the new type), Position (X and Y, ∠, L, Lands At for a platform that plays a note; X and Y dimmed while the piece follows its hit; L and Slope for a straight rail or tunnel), the tie and Advanced (Bounce, Depth, Thickness and the type's other parameters in user words, closed by default). The header subtitle names the tie: "Plays C4 · 0:03.2", "Follows Marble 1 at 0:03.2" or "Free". The tie section is titled Plays, Follows or Free and is one row that selects what the piece is tied to: "Note 1 · B4 · 0:01.00" with the marble's colour dot for a platform, the marble's name and the moment with an anchor for a piece that follows; a free piece shows only the Free title row, with the explanation in its help mark. The Detach icon on the row detaches it in place. The header pill shows the timing with the words of the Timing statuses table in words.md: On Time in green, "12 ms Late" in amber, Missed, Stopped or Blocked in red, with the marble, the note and the reason in its tooltip. Rails and tunnels show what the marble does on them instead (Rides 1.2 s, Still Riding, Missed). Several pieces show the shared fields with Mixed where they differ
- Hit: the header names the hit and its marble with the timing pill; Note edits Pitch (a note name with a semitone stepper; arrows step a semitone, Shift an octave, and a change plays the note), Velocity and Time. Played By is a row that selects the platform, or Add Platform, which places an auto-placed platform on the open dot
- Marble: the colour swatches and Sound, then Release (X, Y); the header pill warns when hits are too close
- Project (nothing selected): Length with Fit to Song (the last note plus 0.4 s, or just past the last marble leaving the frame with a Finish) and Marble Speed (Speed slider with a typeable value; "gravity" only in its tooltip, which says it changes every marble); Film adds Look (theme, colours, Colour by Note, quality, Seamless Loop)
- Song: the file and its kind, tempo and meter when known, Replace Song… and Delete Song, then Backing (its note count and the Sound it plays with)
- Camera: Frame (9:16 Portrait, 1:1, 4:5, 16:9), then Automatic or Manual with Distance, Pitch, Lead and Smooth, the framing problem at the playhead with its fix, the shot count with Add Shot, and Take Over Camera or Reset Camera…; the same in Compose and Film
- Titles (several selected): the titles as links and Add Title

Explanations sit behind a ? icon or in a tooltip, never as text in a panel. The timeline is resizable from its top edge and collapsible; its height is remembered and double-clicking the edge resets it.

## Canvas

The canvas is open like Figma: every element is placed with its tool, moved by dragging and turned with its grip.

- Move (V) selects, drags and turns. Dragging a piece moves it with the pointer every frame while the run behind it re-simulates as a what-if; the trajectory shown is where the marble goes if the piece stays there
- Every what-if says what it does to the run beside the pointer: "Breaks 86 hits" in red or "Breaks no hits", in the drag chip and, before the click, while an armed Windmill or Switchback hovers over a trajectory or a dot, or the Platform tool over a trajectory. The hover shows the piece the click would add and the run with it. A windmill added on a trajectory follows the marble beside its fall, its arms clear of every marble's path and every piece, so adding one keeps the run; dragging it into the path makes the marble turn it
- Drops attach like Figma's snapping, with a chip beside the piece saying what will happen: over a hit dot a platform links (accent ring on the dot, chip "Hit 4 · C5") and plays that note; near a trajectory any piece anchors to that moment (chip with the marble and time); elsewhere it stays free. Hold ⌥ while dragging to drop it free; the magnet toggle in the canvas corner and View › Snap turn snapping off
- Shift keeps a move on one axis and snaps rotation to 5°, 15° while placing
- Marble (O) releases a new marble where clicked and adds its lane. Platform (P) places a free platform on click, or orients it along a drag; on a hit dot it places an auto-placed platform right away. Path (B) draws a rail and U arms the same tool for a tunnel; the drawn path anchors when it starts on a trajectory. Windmill (W) and Switchback, armed from the Insert library, place free on click, or anchor where a trajectory is clicked
- Insert (+, ⇧I) opens a 268 px popover beside the rail like Figma's assets and Paper's insert panel: Paths (Rail, Tunnel) and Pieces (Platform, Windmill, Switchback, Marble) as two-column tiles. Each tile shows its real default geometry from the piece definition on a canvas-coloured well, with the marble and a dashed line for what it does, the name and its key; the one-line description is the tile's tooltip. A click arms the item's tool (rails and tunnels arm Path with that type) and the armed tile has an accent ring; dragging a tile past 4 px closes the popover and shows the piece on the wall under the pointer with the move rules (link to a dot, anchor near a trajectory, ⌥ for free), outside the canvas a small ghost tile follows the pointer. Escape or a press outside closes it. The + button lights up while Windmill or Switchback is armed
- Path (B) draws rails like Figma's pen: click adds a point, dragging pulls symmetric curve handles (⌥ moves only the outgoing one), Return or a click on the first or last point finishes, Backspace removes the last point, Escape cancels. The run follows the rail while drawing. A rail that starts on a trajectory is anchored there. A selected drawn rail shows its points and handles for editing; ⌥ breaks a handle's symmetry
- Clicking an open note's dot opens its insert menu at the dot: Add Platform, Add Platform at Default Tilt, and for a long gap Add Switchback, Add Rail and Add Tunnel, then Go to Note
- The contextual build bar sits under the selected linked piece, or under the next open dot when nothing is selected, like Figma's selection toolbar: Next (Place Next Platform), Repeat Last Platform, Mirror Last Platform, Add Platform and, when the rhythm repeats, Continue Pattern with the count, "Continue Pattern (4)". Its keys work everywhere. It hides while dragging, drawing, playing or when its target is out of view
- Pieces use flat toy colours: platform coral, rail blue, tunnel violet, windmill green. Every piece keeps a visible minimum size at any zoom, and marbles keep a minimum on-screen radius
- Suggestions are ghosts: dashed, see-through pieces with dashed dots; in dark mode the outline is lightened and the fill raised to 40 % of a lighter tint, so a ghost never reads as a solid bar. They hide while an element is dragged
- Pieces the marble has not reached yet at the playhead keep their full colour, so they never read as pale suggestion ghosts; the trajectory after the playhead is drawn quieter and shows what is still to come
- Two-finger scroll pans, pinch zooms, Space and drag pans
- ⌘+ and ⌘− zoom, ⇧1 fits the releases, the pieces and each marble's run up to its last placed hit and the next open dot (not the free fall past it, so tapped hits keep readable dots), ⇧2 fits the selection, ⇧3 zooms to the film frame width around the marble
- Compose has one view, the flat Wall; the film camera shows only in Film's Draft Preview
- Follow sits in the canvas corner beside Snap: a locate glyph (LocateFixed) with an accent fill while on, F, and the tooltip "Follow the marble at the playhead · panning turns it off". It is a toggle with `aria-pressed`, like Snap, and its state shows the moment a hand pan turns it off
- The dot grid fades between densities while zooming
- Projects open in the film-frame view, with the marble in the upper third; the canvas follows the marble only while Follow is on, and a piece placed by an assist glides into view

## Selection and editing

- Click selects, Shift-click adds, dragging on empty canvas draws a marquee, Escape clears
- Selecting a piece moves the playhead to its hit, unless playing, so the piece is never shown ghosted
- Long-gap dots are diamonds with a dashed ring; their insert menu offers Switchback, Rail and Tunnel. Dots outside the frame are red and say by how much
- Delete removes, ⌘A selects all pieces, ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z undo and redo. Duplicate (⌘D, like Figma and Finder) copies the selected pieces as free pieces a little right of and below them, with the next default names; a note has one platform, so a copy never plays it. ⌘D is Don't Save only while the save alert is open. Repeat and Continue copy a piece onto the next hits
- Right-click selects what is under the pointer and opens a context menu with the same commands and shortcuts as the palette: pieces (Add Platform, Rotate, Zoom to Selection, Duplicate, Rename, Delete), open dots (place any piece type, bridge a long gap), marbles and lanes (Add Hit, Mute, Solo, Rename, Delete Marble), timeline hits (Go to Hit, Play from Hit, Select Piece, Move to another marble, Delete) and empty canvas (step loop and view)
- Numeric fields scrub when dragging their label, like Figma, and show units: cm, degrees, ms

In the desktop app WebKit hands a ⌘ key to the page before the menu bar. The studio's key handler runs the command and prevents the default, so the menu bar never runs it a second time; a key the page leaves alone, such as ⌘S or ⌘Z in a text field or the chat, reaches the menu bar, which runs the same command. The menu's Undo, Redo and Select All act on the focused text field's text, like AppKit's. The browser surface has no menu bar and keeps the key handler alone.

Return acts as Next only while something is selected or offered; otherwise it keeps its timeline meaning and jumps to the start. One key has one meaning at a time: the editor that holds the selection takes the key, so with notes selected 0 to 9 belong to the piano roll and 0 leaves the camera alone, like Logic's key focus.

## Timeline

- **Transport:** the header centres one group of plain lucide glyphs at 1.75 stroke, like Logic's control bar: Go to Beginning (skip back, ↩), Previous Hit and Next Hit (step chevrons against a bar, ← →) and Play or Pause (Space). Play has no circle behind it; its glyph is filled and turns green while playing. Tooltips name the command and its shortcut
- **LCD:** beside the transport a recessed readout shows the playhead time in 15 px tabular figures, the second exception to the type scale, with the length in 11 px secondary. Clicking the time turns it into a field with the time selected: Return goes to the typed time (`m:ss.cc`, `m:ss` or seconds, clamped to the length), Escape or leaving the field cancels, and text that is not a time turns the ring red
- **Zoom:** at the right, Final Cut's zoom slider between − and + glyphs on a logarithmic scale, then Zoom to Fit. Pinch or ⌘ with scroll zooms around the pointer. A newly opened project shows its whole length
- Space plays and pauses, Return jumps to the start, Left and Right move to the previous and next hit
- **Song row:** the Song layer, only while the project has a song file or Backing notes, sits on top of the marble rows with an icon tile (waveform for a recording, a note for MIDI), the file name and its kind with the length or tempo. A recording shows its waveform with onset ticks; a MIDI song shows a strip of every marble's notes in their colours, higher notes higher. Right-clicking it offers Replace Song… and Delete Song. Without a file it reads Song with "Backing · 12 notes". The row is absent while the project has no Song layer
- **Layers column:** the same layers as the sidebar's Layers in the same order, like After Effects: Song, one row per marble, then Camera and Titles in Film. Its header reads Layers with the shared Add menu (+). Clicking a row header selects the same layer as its row in Layers. Dropping a MIDI or audio file anywhere on the window imports it, with the accent drop frame
- **Track headers** stick to the left like GarageBand and stay above everything that scrolls, the playhead included: a round colour chip, the name with the instrument and hit count below, and icon toggles for Mute (speaker, crossed out while muted, grey fill) and Solo (headphones, accent fill) while lanes are synthesized. The toggles stay tertiary until the header is hovered or selected, and a muted track dims its name. Mute and solo are view state and change playback immediately
- **Piano roll:** only the Song layer has one, and only while the song has notes; it expands from its disclosure triangle like Logic's piano roll. The header column then shows a keyboard strip, black keys reaching in from the left and C keys labelled with their octave; clicking a key plays it. The lane shows one 12 px row per semitone over the song's range plus two semitones, black-key rows shaded and a line under each C, and notes drawn as blocks whose left edge is the note's time. Dragging a note moves it in time and snaps it to semitones, as one undo step, and plays it at its new pitch on drop. The expanded state is remembered for the session. It shows at most 240 px and scrolls inside, keyboard strip and notes together, so the canvas and the marble tracks stay in view like GarageBand's editor pane; the header keeps the song's name above the strip. A recording stays a flat waveform row; marble tracks are flat rows of diamonds at one height
- The ruler shows minutes and seconds with minor ticks, a red playhead head and a draggable end bracket for the project length, Logic's end marker. Like Logic's ruler it runs on past the end to the window's edge, so the end can be dragged out, but past the end the ruler is shaded like the lanes, Blender's area past the end frame, and its ticks and times are dimmed, so the film visibly stops at the bracket. The separator line between the ruler and the rows is Film's render bar, like Final Cut's: 2 px of green over the frames Live Preview has rendered, brighter at Fast lighting, and the plain separator elsewhere. The playhead stays inside the scrolling area at every zoom: its head and line pass under the Layers column
- Hits are diamonds, Blender's keyframe shape: hollow until a piece plays them, filled in the lane colour when on time, amber-edged when early or late, red when missed, accent yellow when selected, and outlined in the accent when their piece is selected on the canvas. A red ring marks hits closer than 180 ms. Notes in the Song's piano roll show who plays them instead: the marble's colour, Backing in grey
- Hits are dragged like notes in Logic; dragging on empty track space draws a time marquee that selects the hits it spans; a marble's release is dragged like a clip in Final Cut
- **Titles row:** in Film only, after Camera and only when the project has titles: a type icon, "Titles" and the count in the header, each title as a 13 px clip from In to Out with its text, like Final Cut's title clips, accent when selected. Click selects, dragging moves both times, dragging an edge trims, each one undo step; overlapping clips stack in two rows
- **Camera row:** in Film only, 32 px: a camera icon, "Camera" and the shot count in the header, shots as small amber tabs pointing at their time, like Final Cut markers, accent yellow when selected. Add Shot in the Add menu and a double-click on the track add one; a marker is dragged sideways to move it. Compose has no Camera row and Layers in Compose no Camera layer, because Compose is about notes and hits (Round 4)

## Commands

⌘K opens a command palette with every command, like Linear, Raycast and Blender's F3 search. It groups commands by section (File, Edit, View, Playback, Tools, Pieces, Marble, Film; Tools and Pieces only in Compose, Film only in Film). A search also finds the other workspace's commands, after this workspace's and dimmed with "Opens Compose" or "Opens Film", like Raycast's grouped results; choosing one switches workspace first, then runs it. It matches word starts and extra keywords, shows check marks for toggles, dims unavailable commands and draws shortcuts as keycaps. Commands live in one registry that the palette, context menus, the shortcut handlers and the macOS menu bar share, so a command reads and behaves the same everywhere.

**Menu bar.** The desktop app has a real macOS menu bar, laid out in `src/modules/studio/menu-bar.ts` from the registry and built by the host (`src-tauri/src/app/menu.rs`):

- **MidiMarble:** About MidiMarble, Services, Hide MidiMarble ⌘H, Hide Others, Show All, Quit MidiMarble ⌘Q (AppKit's own items). There is no Settings… item while the app has no settings
- **File:** New Project… ⌘N, Open Project… ⌘O, Open Recent ▸ (recent projects newest first, then Clear Menu), Close Project ⌘W, Save ⌘S, Save As… ⇧⌘S, Import Song… ⌘I, Replace Song…, Delete Song, Export Film… ⌘E
- **Edit:** Undo ⌘Z and Redo ⇧⌘Z of the project, Cut, Copy and Paste (AppKit's, for text), Duplicate ⌘D, Delete, Select All ⌘A, Deselect All, Rename…, Make Backing, Show First Problem
- **View:** Compose ⌘1 and Film ⌘2 (checked), Show or Hide Sidebar ⌃⌘S, Inspector ⌃⌘I, Timeline ⌃⌘T and Agent ⌘J, Zoom In ⌘=, Zoom Out ⌘−, Zoom to Fit, Zoom to Selection, Zoom to Film Frame, Snap, Follow Marble in View and Quick Help (checked), Enter Full Screen
- **Window:** Minimize, Zoom, Bring All to Front and the window list (AppKit's)
- **Help:** MidiMarble Help ⇧⌘/ (Quick Help, with AppKit's Help search), Reset Hints

Titles, keys, enabled and checked states come from the registry: Save is dimmed while a saved project has nothing waiting to save, Follow and Snap show their check, the panel items say Show or Hide. Items act in the current workspace and are dimmed where they do not apply, such as Zoom In in Film. Only ⌘ and ⌃ keys are menu keys; single keys (F, ⇧1, ⌫, Space, T) stay with the window, so typing in a text field never runs a menu command, and their items show no key.

## Agents

The agent is a shared chat, like Figma AI and Cursor: the Agent button in the title bar or ⌘J opens a 360 px panel at the right edge, in Compose and in Film. A project has one conversation, made of its agent runs, so it survives a reload the way the runs do.

- Composer: a rounded field with mention chips above the text and a round send button. The current selection appears as chips (a piece icon or a marble's colour dot and the name), follows the selection, and a chip's × leaves it out of this message. Typing @ opens a picker of the Project, the marbles and the pieces above the composer; arrows move, Return or Tab picks, Escape closes. Return sends, Shift-Return adds a line, Backspace at the start removes the last chip
- Sending starts one run per mentioned piece. Marbles and the Project have no asset of their own and share one project run; a message without mentions goes to the Project. Each run's prompt names its target, so the reply line keeps its name after a reload
- The user's message is a right-aligned bubble with its mentions in the accent. Each run answers with one status line: its state glyph, the target name, the state and the step count ("Platform 1 · Working · 14 steps"), a stop button while it works. Clicking the line expands the steps and the agent's text; nothing else is shown by default
- A finished candidate is a card with its 16:10 preview, the label and Discard and Accept. The canvas shows the candidate in place while it is pending. A New Theme Result is a theme card: the theme drawn in its own colours like the Draft Preview (wall, three platforms and a marble), the skin the run made as a small still in its bottom-right corner, then the theme's name with its colour swatches and "Theme · with Sunset Arch" as the quiet second line
- No explanatory subtext. In the browser preview, where no CLI can run, one quiet note above the composer says "Agents run in the desktop app" with the one ? tooltip, and send stays disabled. If no CLI is installed, or a run fails because the CLI is not logged in, a card shows the Terminal command that fixes it, such as `claude /login`, with a copy button

## Film workspace

- Library tiles follow Figma's Assets panel and Final Cut's browser: two columns of square rendered previews (160 px stills of one piece on a neutral wall, or the theme's Jingle Bells still) with a name and a quiet origin line, a search field with the reload button beside it, and one section title with a help mark (Themes, or Platform Skins with a selection). A tile the selection shows gets the accent ring; In Use is a small dark badge in the preview's bottom-left corner; the Keep in Library star sits in the top-right corner, shown on hover and always when kept (filled, accent). New Theme with Agent… is a full-width button below the theme cards
- Blender's state is the title bar's Blender chip; agents at work show in the chat and as a dot on the Agent button; an export shows as a ring in the Export Film… button while the sheet is closed. The preview has no floating Blender button: Open in Blender is in the chip's menu and the command palette
- The Film inspector uses the same grid and has no prose: the Project shows Look (Theme, Quality as Draft, Standard, High or Best with size and frame rate, Seamless Loop with the loop check as one state row and Snap End to Bar when needed), Camera (Automatic or Manual, Distance, Pitch, Lead, Smooth, Shots with Add Shot, Take Over Camera), Titles (a + in the title and one row per title) and Export (the preflight as one state row, Export Film…). A piece shows Look: the skin tile that takes library drops, Skin and Reaction, Apply to All for a custom skin and Blender's link as one row with a dot (Linked, or Detached with the reason and a reset button). There is no agent panel in the inspector
- **Draft Preview.** Whenever Blender's render is not there (the browser, Blender not installed, starting or failed), the preview area shows the run drawn in the app through the same follow camera, like Final Cut's proxy playback: the Compose renderer looking through the film camera, with the theme approximated in flat colours (wall, bar finishes and devices, chrome rails, Colour by Note, titles as text). A quiet "Draft Preview" label with a ? ("Blender draws the real look. This preview follows the same camera.") sits in the frame's top-left corner, a spinner beside it while Blender starts or renders; picking, title placing, library drops, scrubbing and playback work as on the real preview, which replaces it when ready
- Placing a title uses a crosshair cursor and a floating hint with an esc keycap; Escape cancels. A title's inspector has Text, Timing (In and Out as m:ss.cc text fields, the defaults being the film's start and end) and Position
- With several platforms selected, such as a section selected in Layers with Shift-click, the Library's skins apply to all of them; the Platform Skins and Themes help marks say so
- Sheets (Import MIDI, Export Film) drop from the title bar with AppKit-style right-aligned form labels and dialog buttons; Return runs the default button and Escape cancels
## Help and hints

- **Quick Help** follows GarageBand's Quick Help and Logic's Quick Help panel. Each region gets a 1.5 px accent outline and an 11 px label card beside it (a 3 px accent edge, the region name in semibold, one secondary line), placed in the wall's gutters so it covers no controls: the sidebar's and the tool rail's labels stack down the wall's left edge, the title bar's and the inspector's down its right edge, the timeline's in rows along its bottom edge clear of the Quick Help panel, and the wall's own label at its centre. Labels and outlines take no pointer events. The panel is a 260 px popover at the bottom right of the wall: "Quick Help" as its 11 px title with a close button, then the hovered control's name in 13 px semibold with its shortcut as a keycap and its detail in 11 px secondary, or "Point at a control to see what it does". Its text comes from the control's existing tooltip (a Figma-style tooltip, a ? bubble, its `title` or a button's label); there is no second help text per control, only the one line per region in `src/modules/studio/quick-help.ts`. Escape closes it unless a menu or sheet takes Escape first
- **First-run hints** are one calm line in a 30 px popover with the raised surface, the popover shadow, a 7 px accent dot, 13 px text, a × and a caret toward the anchor: right of the Song track header and of the next open dot (so it covers neither the marble nor the build bar), and right of the Compose and Film switch. Elements mark their place with `data-hint-anchor`; a hint waits while its anchor is out of view. One hint at a time, never during Quick Help, no motion beyond a short fade (none with Reduce Motion)
- **Save Changes alert**: a sheet from the title bar like the other sheets, "Save changes to Jingle Bells?" in semibold, one secondary line "Your changes are lost if you don't save them.", then Don't Save at the left and Cancel and Save… (default, Return) at the right, as in macOS

## Visual style

- System font through `-apple-system`, compact density like Finder and Logic
- Type scale: 13 px for body, list rows, field labels and values, panel headers (semibold), menus and buttons; 11 px for secondary text, captions, units, section headers and ruler labels. The palette search field is the one 15 px exception. Numbers use tabular figures of the system font, not a monospaced font
- Brand yellow from the logo (`#FDE200`) as the accent for selection, focus and primary actions, used like the highlight yellow in Photos and Final Cut Pro, with dark text on yellow
- Selection: the active row is filled with the accent and dark text, like a focused macOS source list; other selected rows are tinted. In dark mode the secondary tint is neutral grey, because yellow over dark grey turns olive
- Follows the system light or dark appearance. Every colour is a token in `styles.css` with a dark value; the browser surface paints a window background where the desktop app shows Liquid Glass
- Thin lucide icons at 1.6 to 1.8 stroke in one family; piece icons are drawn to match
- Motion is short and functional; Reduce Motion disables decorative animation

## Controls

- Push buttons are 24 px high; dialog buttons are at least 76 px wide with the default button in the accent, and Return and Escape act on them
- Inspector fields are 24 px high, filled without a border like Figma UI3, with the label inside on the left in 11 px secondary and the unit inside on the right in 11 px tertiary; hover shows a hairline ring and focus an accent ring. Numeric labels scrub on drag and brighten on hover; arrow keys step, Shift steps ten times. Sheets keep 22 px bordered fields
- Relation rows (Plays, Follows, Played By) have a field's shape, a chevron and an optional icon action; state rows (Blender link, loop check, preflight) have a coloured dot, a short label and a ? for the detail
- Status pills are 20 px high, tinted in the status colour with a dot and the label, with the detail in a tooltip
- Pop-up buttons are native selects drawn with the AppKit double chevron. Checkboxes are 14 px with a 3.5 px radius, accent fill and a dark check

- Empty states name the next action with a short title in Title Case, one sentence of help and a button where one fits: no hits shows Add Music with MIDI and Audio at the bottom of the canvas; with open hits the build bar waits under the pulsing dot
- Dropping a file shows an accent drop frame over the window, like Finder and Logic
